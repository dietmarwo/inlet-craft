# 3. Wind, PM mass and response

Positive headwind points toward the rider; crosswind is signed lateral wind. Apparent incoming horizontal components are (riding_speed+headwind,crosswind). Optional `platform.wind_samples` takes wind speed, direction **from** heading (0headwind,90crosswind,180tailwind), and weights, in place of component arrays. Speed weights combine independently with wind weights and are normalized; zero-weight conditions are skipped. No stochastic independence beyond what the user declares is inferred.

The probe's effective angle satisfies cos(theta)=sin(phi)*cos(yaw), with phi the tilt from vertical. Vertical probes therefore see90deg horizontal flow. A horizontal forward-facing probe with a sufficiently strong tailwind sees>90deg and is rejected. The slow-air interpolation uses the orthogonality of vertical settling and horizontal freestream for its vector speed correction; this is a declared geometric extension of the scalar source. Auto uses zero wind→calm, positive wind up to1.5m/s→slow, above1.5→moving. Extension of the slow interpolation below.5m/s is explicitly diagnosed. No calm threshold hides a small nonzero wind.

Each size mode is a **number** lognormal in physical diameter. Supply number concentration, geometric mean physical diameter and geometric standard deviation;1 denotes an exact monodisperse population. All modes share the case's density/shape. PM ratios compare integrated particle mass below ideal2.5/10µm aerodynamic hard cuts with and without the modeled inlet. They do not include the optical response or actual cut curve of a commercial sensor. No assumed mode is a measurement of Aachen's aerosol.

Integration uses the mass-shifted normal in log diameter, truncated at the ideal aerodynamic cut; nine standard deviations cover numerical tails. The exact truncated mass normalizes each mode, and convergence is tested between64 and128 bins. The user can choose16–256 bins. A zero ideal mass returns null efficiency rather than a division by zero. The result records bins and the distribution.

Travel through tubes/arcs/conical tapers is plug delay. Each buffer is an ideally stirred tank with tau=V/Q. `sensor.response_time_s` means first-order **tau**, nott90; convert a measured single-pole t90 by dividing by ln10. A trailing average has mean ageT/2 and varianceT²/12.

`mean_age_s` sums plug delay, tank/sensor time constants and half the averaging interval. Multiplying by riding speed gives `mean_position_offset_m`: the reading belongs to air encountered that distance earlier along the trajectory. Correcting GNSS should use trajectory history at the mean timestamp, not simply shift coordinates on a curved road.

Resolution here means speed times the modeled step's10–90% rise time. It is separate from mean offset. The exact repeated-two-tank response is tested; longer chains use bounded uniformization and the trailing average uses128-point quadrature. More than12 tanks or excessive stiffness/work estimate is rejected before an expensive solve. This numerical cap is not a physical limit of mixing tanks.
